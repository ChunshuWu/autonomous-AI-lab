# Scheduling model

**Goal:** The orchestrator turns team decisions into clear assignments, lets agents sleep when they have nothing to do, and keeps saved work and the dashboard in agreement.

## Run the shared workflow

The orchestrator keeps one current stage, selected question, selected method, and next action per project. Every preparation and review task receives the project’s saved rejected candidates, their human rejection reasons and links to full records, across all earlier meetings. This context must survive worker replacement and session resets. Share concise descriptions once and make complete candidate files available for checking details; never rely on an agent remembering the previous meeting. Follow [research.md](research.md) for scientific choices and [communication.md](communication.md) for meetings and votes.

- **Proposal:** Assign independent preparation, collect candidates, and chair the choice of the next small investment. Route literature questions to researchers and implementation/data/runtime questions to an engineer’s bounded feasibility check. Use the returned evidence in the meeting; do not require another reading cycle for a measurement question.
- **Research:** Supply the selected proposal and the same short research brief with every assignment. Ask each researcher for its own complete method and a non-expert explanation of its implementation choices, using its specialty as a viewpoint rather than a separate goal or isolated work package. Before voting, check that candidates use the same idea, measured outcome and comparable conditions. If the flow was misunderstood, save the researchers’ explanations and the orchestrator’s common brief, ask the human how to proceed; preserve IDs, findings and past votes. Do not merge candidates before the method vote. After the human decision and shared handoff, give the engineer the approved design and agreed tests, and the mathematician any mathematical question. Let waiting researchers sleep, then wake them together to assess results and advise the human on the next action.
- **Return or finish:** Record the human’s decision to continue, return to Proposal, or conclude, and update the dashboard. Preserve alternatives and failed attempts.

For a feasibility check, name one candidate, the uncertainty to resolve, expected resource needs, and a scientific stopping point. Check that this work is already allowed; a vote or an uncertain estimate cannot authorize prohibited experiments or extra budget. The human approves a feasibility check before it runs. Researchers sleep during it, then receive the saved result for the next requested meeting. A negative or inconclusive measurement is still a valid result. The check does not select a proposal or open a new research stage.

Record who is expected at each meeting. Set an expected waiting time; if someone cannot contribute, report the obstacle and resolve participation before voting. Do not invent missing contributions or count silence as a vote.

## Decide, authorize, assign

Each selection has one independent preparation, one discussion and one researcher vote. The engineer and mathematician join the discussion with the complete candidates. There is no automatic revision or repeat vote. The controller may arrange these already agreed steps, but after voting it saves the meeting and enters **waiting for the human director**. Neither a majority, unanimity, a tie nor “none is ready” authorizes a next research action.

The human chooses the candidate and next step in the meeting view. The orchestrator records that choice and shares it with all researchers, the engineer and mathematician, including the complete human–agent conversations. Each participant acknowledges the handoff before dependent work starts. A late answer is shared too. Keep the decision and delivery receipts across restarts. Do not spend model calls polling for a decision; agents sleep and can answer direct questions while waiting. Repairs may restore delivery within the approved scope, but cannot bypass a human decision, change the chosen method or approve a new experiment.

Each assignment names an owner, expected output, input links, dependencies, resource allowance, and stopping point. When moving from Proposal to Research, supply the selected proposal once, its necessary evidence and the new assignment. Leave unselected proposals linked in the map and meeting; do not copy them all into each new assignment. Further work requested by the human receives the relevant candidate and meeting advice. Check the complete new packets before releasing the batch. After an experiment, include its full saved report, the full assessments, and the tested method with its ID. When the engineer continues earlier work, also supply the completed code and data with read-only access. Check the saved delivery and file records before a model call; write new work in a separate task folder. Direct dependencies carry their complete saved documents, not just summaries or file paths another worker cannot open. Every task receives read-only access to the completed work attached to it, regardless of its author’s role. This includes full researcher assessments as well as experiment reports, code, numerical checks and figures. Access follows the completed prerequisites of those inputs, including across a return to Proposal. Verify access before starting; a summary with an inaccessible link is not a complete handoff. Keep unrelated drafts and unfinished work outside this access. Reading these saved files does not authorize new experiments or web searches during a meeting. A follow-up keeps these inputs and earlier findings; send it only to the affected owners and preserve completed peers. Check shared resource allocations before starting. Every registered team member may work in parallel, regardless of role. Give each agent one work task and one separate read-only director reply at a time, instead of a fixed shared worker or reply cap. Wait for real dependencies and shared meeting checkpoints; keep tasks sharing an agent’s research session in order. Use one task ID and one current worker per task. Persist the assignment and its permission before dispatching it; acknowledge receipt so a saved plan is not mistaken for running work. A saved proposal and its completed selection meeting are enough to mark the direction active; a report is not required. This selection does not release research.


## Usage and server resources

Show model calls and reported token use as counters, without threshold warnings or automatic stops. Preserve them across continuation. Coordination checkpoints and elapsed time may raise warnings without stopping work; a warning does not require permission to continue. Keep the one-round meeting and required human decision; stop for the director's instruction, repeated failures, or no useful next action. Repeated discussion without new evidence raises a warning and calls for targeted reading or a useful check.

Experiments have no artificial wall-time, CPU-time, memory or processor quota. Use CPU/GPU and memory actually available on the server, after checking other jobs, free memory, storage and system restrictions. Coordinate shared jobs and choose suitable parallelism; if a job will not fit, batch it, wait, or report the real constraint. Long work may continue while progressing. Save intermediate results and costs, keep status current, and retain pause/cancel controls. Do not kill a healthy job merely because a time estimate was exceeded. Real system limits still apply. No physical experiments or paid external resources are implied.

Use `usage_mode: "warn"` for new task allowances; this legacy setting keeps calls unrestricted, while call and token totals are counters only. Existing saved runs need an explicit policy update at an idle checkpoint, including refreshed scope/resource instructions and the worker snapshot, so old caps do not reappear. Preserve completed evidence and votes; never rerun them to resume. Hard limits apply only when the director explicitly requests them.

## Save and deliver once

Agents use saved requests for handoffs and questions: request ID, sender, recipient, task or question, relevant links, expected response, and status. The dispatcher delivers to the named agent; the orchestrator resolves unclear ownership and overdue replies. Specialists reply with a short answer and evidence links. A question can wake a specialist without starting unrelated work.

For a result handoff:

1. The producing agent saves the real output, settings, evidence, and a short summary under its task ID.
2. The dispatcher saves the matching dashboard records and timeline entry, linked to those files and the same task ID.
3. Confirm that the files are available and the dashboard accepted the record ID and version. Only then mark the handoff delivered and release dependent work. For source-reading work, also confirm the paper records and project links under the library model before opening the dependent meeting. A delivered proposal note alone does not complete the source handoff.

Writing targets and dashboard formatting must not block a valid result. Accept complete saved answers even when longer than requested. Convert clear record formats automatically. If a chart or detailed record cannot be displayed, save the complete original result and a readable dashboard document in the same handoff, show a display-repair warning, and let peers assess it. Keep missing evidence, changed permissions, invalid votes and failed scientific work separate; these still need resolution. If the result itself or its readable dashboard record is not saved, keep the handoff pending and retry the missing part using the same ID. Do not rerun an experiment or create duplicate records because delivery failed. Keep scientific outcome separate from delivery: negative results and failed tests are valid outputs. A complete provisional candidate can be delivered during preparation while its readiness remains uncertain. Confirm its source handoff before opening peer review. Prefer reading that resolves a specific decision over repeating the same discussion. The orchestrator may send it to a vote with those limits intact; voters use none_is_ready when no candidate justifies a useful, affordable next step. Uncertainty the step is meant to resolve is not, by itself, a reason to reject it. Never turn missing inputs into invented content or treat delivery as scientific acceptance.

## Report your own status

The task service records working, finished, or blocked status from actual worker events. Agents outside that service use `researcher_action` with `action: "status"` at task changes and before ending its turn. Include its own `agent_id`, `task_id`, a unique `client_id`, the saved `status_revision` as `expected_revision` (0 initially), `work_state` (`working`, `sleeping`, `blocked`, or `finished`), and a brief `summary`. For sleep or a block, include `waiting_for`: the event or help needed. Read back the saved status; retry an unchanged update with the same `client_id`.

For research, claim released work before reporting `working`. The assigned orchestrator reports coordination with `work_kind: "coordination"` under the enabled task allowance; it does not need to release research or clear a research hold. Reporting sleep, a block, or completion stops active work; it does not approve the next task. The dashboard keeps the reported state and last check-in separate. A long silence makes running work uncertain; sleeping or finished work does not expire. Update at meaningful checkpoints, not through repeated model calls. The orchestrator checks missed final updates and assignment mismatches.

## Sleep and wake

When researchers have finished preparation and are waiting for results, save their findings, open questions, and the event they await; then end their model turns. Sleep means **no model calls**, repeated status checks, or extra reading just to stay busy. Apply this to other idle roles, including the orchestrator.

A small service watches task events without calling a model. Wake only the needed agents when results reach the agreed checkpoint, a direct question or meeting request arrives, a blocking failure needs their decision, or the director gives an instruction. After a short reply, an agent can return to sleep; answering does not lift a research hold. Give director questions priority. Different agents can answer at the same time, alongside their research; keep one read-only reply per agent at a time. An agent may answer from saved evidence while its research runs; use a fresh, read-only reply session that does not overwrite its research session or change its task. Keep replies for the same agent in order. If the answer needs unfinished results, say what is known and what is pending.

Sleeping preserves the role and its cat identity; it does not dismiss the agent. The experiment may keep consuming computing resources while researchers sleep.

## Avoid wasted model calls

- Read all five models when joining. The service freezes their text for the project run; live skill edits do not change active workers. Adopt a new instruction version only at a saved checkpoint with no active tasks. Reuse bounded research sessions. Meetings, votes and review questions use fresh sessions with complete task evidence, the agent’s specialty and its saved research handoff, without the full browsing history. After any reset, provide the current task and all evidence needed for its decision; the working summary alone is not enough. Do not recreate agents for each small question.
- Keep shared instructions before changing task details. Include repeated scope text once, with a clear reference, only when the copies are identical; preserve complete scientific evidence and the original saved packet. Tell workers which tools, network access and worker capacity they actually have, so they avoid known failed commands and stale resource assumptions. Supply current director writing preferences with new tasks, separately from the saved research models. These updates do not change scientific scope or require a session reset.
- Batch related questions and routine results at useful checkpoints. Wake only the needed specialists, and exchange short answers with evidence links instead of full transcripts.
- Use the catalog or a script for simple location lookups, status checks, and delivery retries. These do not need a new model turn. Routine replies do not need the highest reasoning setting; scientific work should use enough reasoning for the task.
- Track per-task token use separately from cumulative session totals. Neither total is current context size; do not add repeated session totals or use them to trigger resets. Warn on the dashboard when repeated startup, large context, or unproductive discussion wastes tokens. Session reuse does not make context free; measure savings before claiming them.

## Recover or ask for help

Whenever an agent is blocked, the service wakes the orchestrator once to inspect the cause and saved work. The orchestrator attempts a specific repair within the existing scope, or asks the right specialist for help, then checks that the affected step really continues. Preserve completed work and peer results; do not repeat the same failed attempt without a changed input or a new diagnosis. If the repair fails or needs missing data, access, or human action, send one clear dashboard request stating what was tried and exactly what is needed. A pause or changed permission still takes priority. Keep polling free of model calls.

This applies to every operational block, including failed input checks before a worker starts, source registration, file access, missing handoff documents, delivery, implementation and the recovery task itself. The orchestrator owns the case until the affected step is verified running or one precise external dependency remains. A saved diagnosis alone does not resolve a block. Ordinary waiting for a human meeting decision or an actual director hold is not a repair target.

Before escalating to the director:

1. Read the exact error, original assignment, saved input files, complete relevant results and delivery receipts. The service supplies these with verified file hashes and the relevant current catalog records, including records not yet linked to the project. Save source-collection inputs even when validation fails before a task claim. Check that the orchestrator can actually read them; a path without access is insufficient.
2. If WuLab omitted an input, access or recovery action, repair that handoff first. The service or orchestrator retrieves existing internal files and records. Do not ask the director to retrieve material already held by WuLab. An empty list of controller actions is an operating limitation to diagnose, not proof that human help is needed.
3. Attempt a concrete repair already permitted by the scope. Use ordinary code for a verified metadata, format, access or delivery repair. Ask the librarian to resolve source identities, the engineer to resolve code/data execution and the mathematician for a mathematical ambiguity. Supply the specialist with the full evidence and a specific question; keep scientific interpretation with researchers. Do not invent evidence or overwrite a genuine version conflict.
4. Retry only the affected step after its input or implementation actually changes. Preserve original files, completed peer work, results and the recovery history. Keep one persisted case and one current repair owner; do not create duplicate workers or repeat an unchanged attempt.
5. Check the real outcome: saved files, matching dashboard receipt, resumed task or downstream checkpoint, and the affected agent's current status. A queued repair or a delivered answer is not verified recovery. Route new scientific results through the normal assessment and human decision.
6. Request human help only for the exact missing external file, unavailable access, new permission or scientific decision that remains after useful permitted checks and repair attempts. State what was inspected, what was attempted, its outcome and why the team cannot resolve the remaining dependency. If repair needs a controller/service change, complete and validate the permitted change before escalating any remaining deployment requirement. Never lift a hold or choose a research direction to make a block disappear.

For a stopped or partial Research implementation, return the concrete repair to the experiment's engineer before escalating to the director. Supply the original implementation, code, selected method and full assessments. Adopt an existing continuation instead of creating a duplicate; persist one owner recovery per original implementation. Check its actual saved result. Send new completed repair evidence through the ordinary researcher assessment before concluding or assigning another experiment. If the owner demonstrates a remaining external obstacle, request that exact input or specialist help with the attempted repairs attached. Do not restart an unchanged failure. A valid negative experiment does not trigger recovery merely because it failed to improve the score. Holds, cleared projects and selection-only demonstration endpoints take priority.

After a recovery is verified, reconcile the affected agent's current status as well as the case and request. An idle agent whose displayed block belongs to that resolved task sleeps until its next assignment. Retain the original failed task and status in history. Do not overwrite active or queued work, a newer block, an unresolved dependency, or a director hold, and do not fabricate a fresh agent check-in. This status cleanup uses ordinary code rather than another model call.

Set expected waiting times and bounded retries with each round. Elapsed time warns; it does not stop healthy work. Keep source searches focused on unresolved questions. Check a late task’s actual status and saved output before retrying; never start a second copy while the first may still run. Resume from confirmed progress after interruption. The controller may repair a known delivery or input-format problem automatically when the fix is specific, the original permission still holds, and the action cannot repeat research or change a scientific judgment. Record the repair and verify delivery. Retry once per identified cause; an unchanged failure needs a new diagnosis. Earlier repairs do not prevent recovery from a different, verified input problem. A missing scientific input goes to the orchestrator with the saved evidence, not through a blind restart.

Pause only the affected work when a dependency, source, resource, or decision is missing. A saved provisional proposal with uncertain novelty can still go to peer review. When preparation itself is blocked, let the orchestrator assign bounded, focused follow-up to the affected owners and preserve completed peers. Fix missing review inputs before retrying only interrupted reviews; never retry unchanged missing evidence. Show its reason and requested help on the dashboard. Repeated meetings without new evidence, repeated failed reading cycles, or undelivered results should raise one concise warning with the observed symptom and proposed action.

## Use the saved task service

The orchestrator uses `lab_tasks` to submit an assignment with a stable `id`, `sender_id`, `agent_id`, `kind`, short `prompt` and `summary`, relevant `input_refs`, and `depends_on` task IDs. Use `kind: "vote"` for ballots, with `decision_packet: {criteria, candidates: [{id, task_id}]}` naming the final saved presentations. The service waits for them and supplies their full texts and versions on every vote. Use `document_ids` for complete saved task documents. The service checks every ready task with the full fresh-session instructions before claiming it, even if its research session can resume. Supply the needed source records as readable content, alongside complete candidates and critiques; a path alone is not evidence for a worker without file tools. Missing evidence stops before a model call. For every task, warn when evidence, instructions, or the full input exceeds 1 MB (1,000,000 bytes); these thresholds signal cost; they do not block work or measure the model’s context limit. Keep required evidence complete and remove duplicate or superseded copies. An actual model input-limit error needs a revised packet before retrying; never silently cut evidence or retry the same rejected input unchanged. Any agent can send a focused `question`. Read the compact queue with `action: "read"`; use `get` for one full result. A director-approved allowance sets scope and research permission; model-call counts are counters only by default; it is not permission to remove a project hold.

For a prepared library handoff, use `kind: "library"`, `handler: "library_delivery"`, the librarian as owner, and one `input_refs` path to the saved JSON inside the project workspace. The service checks and delivers that file without a model call; ordinary `handler: "model"` remains for work needing reasoning.

The service claims each validated task, runs its model or script, saves the answer and files, and confirms dashboard delivery before releasing dependent tasks. Workers return a brief `body` and optional Markdown `document` for longer detail. The service saves the document as `reply.md` and makes it readable in the dashboard. Outside controller-managed rounds, a worker can return focused peer requests; their complete saved replies and documents wake the requester. Within a managed round, save missing-input requests in the result for the checkpoint; do not start peer-request chains. Use `notify_orchestrator` for a checkpoint needing its attention, not for every small update. Director questions on dashboard records and reports enter the same queue.

Polling and idle waiting use no model calls. Allow every registered team member to work at once, using one work task and one read-only reply per agent. Only registered team members get workers. Keep retries bounded; never repeat model work just to retry delivery. A failed task stops its dependents. The orchestrator checks the saved output and cause before retrying; do not disguise repeated failures as new tasks. A growing queue, lost workers, and excessive context raise dashboard warnings.

The local service must be running and connected, with the project in its configuration. Confirm online search for source-reading tasks; meetings and votes use the supplied evidence. Check its last contact before promising automatic wake-ups. The workflow controller is ordinary code. It saves the current stage, participants, task dependencies and human checkpoint. It advances the agreed preparation, discussion and vote only after complete saved results and source registration. After voting or result review, it waits for the director. A prose answer, stale decision, repair or model-generated plan cannot release that wait.

For a new run, configure `lab_workflow` with researcher IDs, librarian, engineer, mathematician, resource brief and expected waiting time. Meetings always use one round. The controller handles independent preparation, adviser and peer discussion, one researcher ballot, the human decision, confirmed shared handoff, approved implementation and result review. Researchers save real source records as `sources.json`; `library_collect` checks and registers them through the librarian without a model call before a meeting. Existing projects can adopt these meeting rules without deleting earlier findings; let active work save before changing pinned worker instructions.
